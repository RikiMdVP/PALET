I want to conduct a systematic review and empirical synthesis on how digital environments and echo chambers interact with populist dynamics to shape the winner–loser gap in democratic attitudes among young citizens in Europe.

Dimensions of analysis
	1.	The populist winner–loser gap: How electoral victories or defeats of populist vs. non-populist parties influence citizens’ satisfaction with democracy and institutional trust.
	2.	Youth and volatility: How younger cohorts display higher volatility in democratic support, particularly after electoral losses.
	3.	Digital echo chambers: Whether digital intensity (online political participation, social media use) amplifies the emotional and cognitive asymmetries between winners and losers.
	4.	Regional context: The moderating role of regional digital climate, populist vote share, and economic inequality in shaping these effects.
	5.	Causal mechanisms: How exposure to populist discourse online may turn individual electoral defeat into broader democratic disaffection.
	6.	Policy relevance: Implications for democratic resilience, media literacy, and youth participation in the EU.
	7.	Knowledge gaps: Need for integrated frameworks linking digitalization, populism, and regional democratic health.

Scope: European Union, with a focus on regional (NUTS-2) and multilevel analyses combining individual (ESS Round 10) and contextual (Eurostat, CHES, PopuList, ParlGov, GPS, and digital intensity) data.
